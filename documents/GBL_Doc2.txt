2. Glassblowing - Document 2: Shaping and Finishing Techniques

After gathering, shaping the glass requires a mix of breath control and tool manipulation. 
The artisan blows gently into the pipe while constantly rotating it to inflate the molten glass into a bubble. 
Once the bubble reaches a desired size, various shaping tools come into play.
Special glassblowing scissors, called “jacks,” are used to pinch and shape the glass neck, while wooden blocks soaked in water help smooth and round the surface.
A blowtorch may be applied to reheat specific sections for adjustments or to attach additional glass elements, such as handles or decorative patterns.
To finish, the piece is detached from the pipe using a technique called “cracking off.” 
A small thermal shock is introduced at the attachment point, and the glass piece is then placed in an annealer to cool gradually over several hours, preventing stress fractures.
