4. Hand Glove Making - Document 2: Stitching and Lining Techniques

Once leather pieces are cut, assembly begins with stitching. Two primary stitching methods are used: “piqué” for exposed seams and “inseam” for hidden seams. Stitching requires strong thread, typically polyester or silk, and uniform tension for durability and aesthetics.

For lined gloves, materials like silk or cashmere are attached inside for comfort and warmth. The lining is often hand-stitched to prevent bulk and ensure a snug fit. Thumb insertion is one of the trickiest steps—requiring precision cuts and curved stitching to maintain flexibility without excess material.

Finally, gloves are blocked on heated metal forms to shape them and smooth out wrinkles, giving the finished product its refined appearance.
