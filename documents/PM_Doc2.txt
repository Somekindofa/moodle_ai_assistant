6. Porcelain Making - Document 2: Glazing and Kiln Firing

After shaping and drying, porcelain undergoes bisque firing to remove moisture and harden the clay. The next step is glazing, which not only decorates the piece but also creates a waterproof surface. Glazes are mixtures of silica, fluxes, and colorants, applied by dipping, brushing, or spraying.

Once glazed, the piece goes through a second firing in a kiln at temperatures exceeding 1,200°C. This stage is called “glost firing.” At this temperature, the glaze vitrifies, forming a glass-like coating. Even heat distribution in the kiln is crucial to prevent warping or glaze defects such as pinholes or crazing.

Cooling must be gradual to avoid thermal shock, which can crack the porcelain.
