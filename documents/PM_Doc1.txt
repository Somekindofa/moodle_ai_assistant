5. Porcelain Making - Document 1: Preparing and Throwing Clay

Porcelain making starts with kaolin clay, which is refined for purity and plasticity. 
The clay must be wedged thoroughly to eliminate air bubbles that could cause cracks during firing.

Throwing is done on a potter’s wheel. The artisan centers the clay lump while the wheel spins at controlled speed. 
Centering is crucial because even slight misalignment will cause instability in shaping. 
Once centered, the artisan uses both hands to open the clay and gradually pull it upward to form the basic wall of the object.

Throughout the process, water is applied to reduce friction, but too much water can weaken the clay. 
Precision in wall thickness is essential for uniform firing and strength.
